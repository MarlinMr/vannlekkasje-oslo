# Grønland district-heating pipe rupture — Oslo, 4 November 2026

On Wednesday evening, 4 November 2026, a pressurized district-heating pipe operated by Hafslund Celsio ruptured beneath the street in the Grønland area of central Oslo, near Tøyenbekken/Grønlandsleiret. It was a failure in the **district-heating network**, not an ordinary drinking-water-main break. Very hot water and steam forced their way through the asphalt, producing a towering plume and throwing stones, soil and broken road material into the air. Hafslund later said the water was above 100°C under pressure, the damaged pipe was about 20 cm in diameter, and more than **one million litres** of water escaped. The precise cause of the rupture was not known as of 5 November. [1][2]

## Sequence of events (local Oslo time)

- **About 17:15–17:17, Wednesday:** Emergency services received reports of water and steam or smoke rising from the roadway. Police, firefighters and Hafslund personnel responded. Steam and water obscured the street; witnesses described loud noises, a sudden cloud, flying debris and people running from the area. [1][3][4]
- **Early response:** Police cordoned off roughly 150 metres around the site, warned people to move away and close their windows, and sent an emergency alert to people nearby. Police estimated that stones weighing 2–3 kg were thrown as far as 20–30 metres. More than 100 people were evacuated from streets, restaurants, shops and the Grønland torg area. Reports differed on the exact number of people in the wider affected area. [1][3][4]
- **At about 18:45 (reported around 19:00):** Hafslund isolated the damaged section and stopped the leak. The rupture left a substantial hole in the road; estimates of its size differed (NRK quoted approximately 5 m², while TV 2 quoted approximately 5 m × 2 m). Workers then excavated the site to inspect and repair the pipe. [1][2][4]
- **Wednesday night:** The shutdown disrupted district-heating circulation across parts of Oslo. Hafslund estimated that approximately **1,500 residential units** around Grønland would be without district heat, affecting hot water and heating overnight. The larger network experienced interruptions during the evening. Grønland metro station was temporarily closed; buses 37 and 60 were diverted. [1][2][4]
- **Thursday morning, 5 November:** Hafslund reported normal district-heat delivery again at approximately 05:30; some taps might initially run cool while systems warmed up. Crews continued securing/insulating the repaired pipe and dealing with the damaged roadway. By later Thursday, public transport was largely normal and the scene was fenced off. [5][6]

There were **no reported serious injuries**; police and firefighters stressed that the absence of injuries was fortunate given the heat and flying rocks. One witness told NRK that a stone struck the back of his head, but said he was all right. Police and firefighters also tried to keep water out of adjacent buildings, though the extent of water intrusion was initially unclear. Hafslund said it would investigate the cause and review its response; its spokesperson acknowledged that high SMS volume delayed some customer notifications. [1][4][6]
